# Writing for Criminal Defense

**A one-semester law school writing course built on a real case**
Jeff Newberry

> **About this syllabus.** This is a public version of the syllabus for a writing course I teach. I have removed the school-specific material, the calendar dates, and every detail that would identify the case or the people in it. Other teachers are welcome to borrow from it and adapt it.

---

## The Case

On the first night of class, you get a client. He is charged with a felony in a Texas district court, he says he is innocent, and his trial is set. The case is real. The record is the one the clerk’s office keeps, and no appellate brief was ever filed in it, which means that by the end of the semester you will have written the brief that nobody wrote.

But you will not start with the record. You will start the way a defense lawyer starts: with a file, a client, and questions. This semester the witnesses in the case (your client, the lawyer who tried the case, and the people who were there) are available to you in the Interview Room, a website built for this course. They talk back. They answer what you ask and nothing more; a vague question gets a vague answer, and a question you forget to ask is a fact you do not get. Your interview time is limited, as it is in practice, so you will plan your interviews before you walk in.

Over fourteen weeks you will represent him at every stage a criminal defense lawyer sees. Before trial, you will attack a search warrant for his phone. After the verdict, you will investigate what went wrong, draft the affidavits that prove it, and move for a new trial. Then you will examine his trial lawyer under oath at a hearing on that motion, in class, with me on the bench. Only when the motion is denied will you get the record: the full trial transcript, the clerk’s file, and the transcript of the hearing you just conducted. From it you will write a statement of facts and a point of error for the appeal.

Five documents, each one a real thing a defense lawyer writes, each one built on facts you found yourself.

## The Interview Room

You will get a sign-in and PIN in the second class. A few things to know:

- The witnesses are played by an AI built for this course. You interview them by typing or by speaking. You do not need to buy or sign up for any AI product.
- Interview time is limited and counts only while someone is talking: 45 minutes total for the pretrial witnesses, and 2½ hours total for the post-trial witnesses. A clock on the screen shows what you have left. Plan your questions before you start; there is no restart button.
- Your interviews are saved to the class site and follow you from device to device. I can read them, and I will. Keep your own notes too; they will become your facts.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

- plan and conduct a witness interview that produces the facts a motion needs;
- draft a motion to suppress attacking a warrant’s particularity;
- draft affidavits and a motion for new trial that would survive a hearing;
- read a trial record and identify preserved and unpreserved error; and
- write a statement of facts and an appellate point of error in Bluebook and Greenbook form.

## Books and Materials

You need in-class access to the *Bluebook* (22d ed. 2025) and the *Greenbook: Texas Rules of Form* (16th ed. 2024). You need the current editions.

Everything else (the pretrial file, the record, and the materials for each assignment) comes from me when the case reaches that stage. You will not get the record early; part of the course is working without it.

You will submit your work as Microsoft Word documents. You will also need your Westlaw or Lexis account in class; I will use Westlaw on the screen, but either is fine.

## Grading and Assessment

Your grade is based on five writing assignments. Final drafts are due by 11:59 p.m. on the due date, as Word documents, by email to me.

| Assignment | Due | Weight | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motion to suppress | Monday, Week 4 | 20% | Pretrial |
| Supporting affidavits (two) | Monday, Week 7 | 15% | Post-trial |
| Motion for new trial | Monday, Week 8 | 25% | Post-trial |
| Statement of facts | Monday, Week 13 | 20% | Appeal |
| Point of error (claim) | About two weeks after the last class | 20% | Appeal |

### Rough drafts

Four of the five assignments have a rough-draft date on the docket below. Rough drafts are not graded, but they are required: on each editing day you will trade drafts with a classmate and edit each other’s work, and there is nothing to edit if you have not written one. Rough drafts are due by 3:00 p.m. on their dates, by email.

### Participation

I expect you to be present and prepared for every class. Besides working through issues together, researching together, and editing each other’s work, you will be asked to think out loud. I reserve the right to raise or lower your final grade by one unit (a B to a B+, for example) based on exceptional participation in either direction.

## AI and Your Work

The words in your motions, affidavits, statement of facts, and point of error must be yours. You may not use generative AI to write or rewrite any of them. There are three exceptions:

- **The Interview Room.** The witnesses you interview are played by an AI, and interviewing them is a required part of this course. Your interviews, the transcripts of them, and your notes from them are your investigation.
- **Research.** You may use the AI research tools the law school provides to you (Lexis+ AI, CoCounsel, and Westlaw’s AI tools) to find and understand authority, in class and on your own. We will work with these tools together. Every authority you cite must be read and verified by you in the primary source, and the analysis and the writing stay yours.
- **Proofreading.** You may use the spelling, grammar, and clarity tools built into Word to check text you have already written.

## Docket

Twenty-eight classes, two a week, in three phases. Rough drafts are due at 3:00 p.m., final drafts at 11:59 p.m., both by email. Reading is listed where it is due. *What happens in each phase depends on what you found in the one before.*

### Pretrial

*Your client is out on bond. Trial is set for August. In discovery, the State has produced a search warrant for his phone, the detective’s affidavit, the return, and an extraction report, and it has marked items from the phone as trial exhibits.*

| Class | Setting | Filed / due |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1, Class 1** | Introduction. The syllabus. The case and the client. Citation refresher. | |
| **Week 1, Class 2** | Citation exercises. The Interview Room on the big screen: how it works, and how to question a witness who answers only what you ask. Sign-ins and PINs issued. | |
| **Week 2, Class 1** | Reading the warrant file together. Where overbreadth hides. Pretrial interviews open (45-minute clock). | Read the warrant file before class. |
| **Week 2, Class 2** | Lecture: motions to suppress. Particularity, overbreadth, severance, good faith. Examples, good and bad. | |
| **Week 3, Class 1** | Research session: the AI research tools, how to verify what they give you, and the authorities for your motion. The fact section of a motion. | Rough motion to suppress due Wednesday, 3:00 p.m. |
| **Week 3, Class 2** | First half: edit a classmate’s rough draft. Second half: the hearing you would get, and what you would ask the detective. Q&A. | **Motion to suppress due Monday of Week 4, 11:59 p.m.** |

### Post-Trial

*The jury has returned its verdict. Your client has been sentenced. You have thirty days to file a motion for new trial, and you do not yet know what went wrong.*

| Class | Setting | Filed / due |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 4, Class 1** | The verdict: what happened at trial, as your client tells it. Post-trial witnesses open (2½-hour clock). Start with your client and trial counsel; who to interview after that, and why. | |
| **Week 4, Class 2** | Lecture: motions for new trial. Rule 21, timing, grounds, ineffective assistance as a ground, and what has to be sworn. | |
| **Week 5, Class 1** | What the interviews are turning up. Which facts need an affidavit, and from whom. | |
| **Week 5, Class 2** | Lecture: supporting affidavits. Form, personal knowledge, what a judge discounts. Examples. | |
| **Week 6, Class 1** | Research session for the motion for new trial: *Strickland* and the Texas cases on the grounds you are raising. | |
| **Week 6, Class 2** | First half: edit a classmate’s affidavit. Second half: structuring the motion around the affidavits. | **Affidavits due Monday of Week 7, 11:59 p.m.** Rough motion for new trial due the same Monday, 3:00 p.m. |
| **Week 7, Class 1** | First half: edit a classmate’s rough motion for new trial. Second half: the prayer, the request for a hearing, and the proposed order. | |
| **Week 7, Class 2** | Motion for new trial procedure after filing: presentment, the hearing, the ruling. Q&A. | **Motion for new trial due Monday of Week 8, 11:59 p.m.** |
| **Week 8, Class 1** | The record is released. How to read a record. Planning your examination of trial counsel. | **Record released.** |
| **Week 8, Class 2** | Hearing on the motions for new trial. Each of you examines trial counsel on the record. Motion denied from the bench. Hearing transcript released the next day. | **Hearing.** |
| **Break week** | Read the record. | Clerk’s record and reporter’s record vols. 1–3 by Week 9, Class 1. Vols. 4–5 and the hearing transcript by Week 9, Class 2. |

### On Appeal

*The motion has been denied. Notice of appeal is filed. The record is on your desk, and the brief is due in the court of appeals.*

| Class | Setting | Filed / due |
|---|---|---|

| **Week 9, Class 2** | Record check-in: what is jumping out. Reading the charge and the judgment. | |

| **Week 10, Class 2** | Issue-spotting, continued. Matching each of you to an issue. | Email me your issue by Friday, 6:00 p.m. (I may adjust it.) |
| **Week 11, Class 1** | Statements of fact: what not to do. What facts your claim needs. Citing the record. | Fact outline due Wednesday, 6:00 p.m. |
| **Week 11, Class 2** | First half: discussion of your fact outlines. Second half: research session for the claims. | Rough statement of facts due Monday of Week 12, 3:00 p.m. |
| **Week 12, Class 1** | First half: edit a classmate’s rough statement of facts. Second half: research session, continued. | |
| **Week 12, Class 2** | Harm analysis under Rule 44.2 and *Almanza*. Outlining the argument. | **Statement of facts due Monday of Week 13, 11:59 p.m.** Claim outline (headings only) due the same Monday, 6:00 p.m. |
| **Week 13, Class 1** | Discussion of outlines. Persuasive headings. | |
| **Week 13, Class 2** | The summary of the argument. Writing the standard of review into the argument. | |
| **Week 14, Class 1** | Conclusions and prayers. Finishing the brief: table of contents, index of authorities, certificates. Q&A. | Rough argument due Wednesday, 3:00 p.m. |
